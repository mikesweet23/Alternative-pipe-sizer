How to use this

five steps
  1. Set the pump arrangement. Duty, duty/standby or duty/assist/standby, and the control mode. Then press Recommend a pump — it sizes against the balanced system and tells you the duty per pump. Type your actual pump into Design flow and head to test what you can really source.
  2. Choose the valve on each consumer. Two-port, two-port with a bypass, three-port or PICV. This is what decides where the flow goes when a load switches off.
  3. Balance it. Calculate balancing works out the regulating drop and Kv each branch needs to hold design flow. That column is your commissioning schedule, and the branch showing open is the index.
  4. Switch consumers off and watch. Flow, head, pump speed and the diagram all re-solve live. Add bypasses where flow needs to keep moving.
  5. Run the part-load sweep. It steps the whole turndown range and names the first point something breaks — below pump minimum, starved branches, over-flow.
Sense checks worth making
  • Every open branch between 85% and 118% of design once balanced.
  • Velocities inside your limits — red is too fast, amber too slow for a main that has lost load.
  • Control valve authority above 0.3, or a PICV holding.
  • Flow never below the pump minimum, whatever shuts down.
  • Pump duty sensible for the job — if the head looks high, read the where the head goes table and see which section is eating it.

Bypasses

end-of-line, on mains or consumers

A bypass across the ends of a line keeps water moving when the two-port valves shut. Set to Only as consumers shut it behaves like a differential-pressure or minimum-flow valve — closed while the consumers are drawing, opening just enough to hold the set flow as they close. Leave the flow blank for 10% of that line's design flow.

Use a real pump curve

Take the duty below to a manufacturer’s selection, then paste the published curve back here — one flow, head pair per line, from shut-off down to run-out. Everything on this page then solves against the real machine instead of the generic parabola.

Where these three figures come from
Shut-off head is the head the pump makes at zero flow — the left-hand end of its curve — as a percentage of the duty head. Read it straight off a manufacturer’s curve. Most inline and end-suction pumps sit between 115% and 140%; a flat curve is nearer 115 and a steep one nearer 140. 125% is a fair stand-in. Paste a real curve below and this stops being a guess.
Assist cut-in and cut-out only matter with more than one pump running, and they are a control setting rather than something you read off a curve — you choose them. Bring the second pump in when one can no longer cover demand comfortably: for an equally split pair that is around 50% of design flow. Drop back out lower, around 35%, so the two thresholds cannot chase each other. Keep 15 to 20 points between them — that gap is the whole point, and too small a one makes the set hunt.
A centrifugal pump runs best near 80% of its rated flow and should not sit below about 25% for long.

These are starting points, not a selection.

Assumptions

Component resistances use standard velocity-head factors (CIBSE C / Crane TP-410) rather than manufacturer data: butterfly 0.35, gate 0.15, strainer 2.5, regulating set 3.0, check 2.0. Control valves are sized to the authority target above. Replace with selected products before issue.
